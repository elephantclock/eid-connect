For your developers

A few lines of code.

The service streams each card read to your app over local HTTP. Any language works; this is JavaScript.

import { connect } from '@elephantclock/eid-connect';

connect({
  onCard: (card) => {
    form.name.value   = card.nameEnglish;
    form.nameAr.value = card.nameArabic;
    form.eid.value    = card.idNumber;
    photo.src         = card.photoDataUrl;
  },
});

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between reading and validating an Emirates ID?

Reading copies the data stored on the card chip, offline. Validating checks online with ICP that the card is genuine, not cancelled and not expired. Many counters only need reading; regulated eKYC needs validation.

Does EID Connect validate the card with ICP?

Yes. Online validation through ICP's validation gateway is available once your organisation completes ICP onboarding, and we guide you through it. It can be switched on or off per counter or per transaction.

Do you support fingerprint verification?

Yes, on request. With a supported fingerprint reader, EID Connect confirms that the person presenting the card is its owner.

Is there a mobile app?

Yes. An Android app reads Emirates IDs over NFC and is in daily use at Citizen Group. iOS is available on request.

Which card readers are supported, and do you supply them?

Standard smart-card and NFC readers that work with Emirates ID. We supply supported readers as part of a bundle with the licence and installation, or recommend models if you prefer to buy your own.

Does it work with our existing system?

Yes. Any web application can receive card data, and we integrate with CRMs, hospital, hotel and property-management systems, and ERPs.

Do we need to install anything in the browser?

No. A small Windows service runs on the counter PC, and your web app connects to it directly.

Is Emirates ID data stored?

The service itself stores nothing. Your application decides what to keep, and we help you keep the minimum needed under UAE data-protection law.
